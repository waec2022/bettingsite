      AVIATOR STRATEGY CENTRE

      Understanding multipliers

      The multiplier is the number your stake is multiplied by when you cash out. At 2.00x a stake of 100 returns 200. It starts at 1.00x and keeps climbing until the round ends, so a higher multiplier means a bigger return but also a longer wait for a result that you cannot predict.

      Understanding crash games

      In a crash game the multiplier rises until it suddenly crashes at a random point. If you cash out before the crash you keep stake times multiplier. If it crashes first, the stake is lost. Each round is independent: past results do not change the next one, and no pattern can be relied on.

      Virtual betting examples

      Example 1: stake 100, cash out at 1.50x, you receive 150 (profit 50). Example 2: stake 100, aim for 5.00x, the plane crashes at 3.20x, you lose 100. Example 3: two bets, one cashed early at 1.40x and one left for a bigger target. These are virtual coins only.

      Risk management

      Decide a limit before you play and stick to it. Use small stakes compared with your balance, never chase losses with bigger stakes, and take breaks. In real betting, never stake money you cannot afford to lose. If gambling stops being fun, seek help from a support service.

      Understanding volatility

      Volatility is how much results swing. Cashing out early at low multipliers gives small, frequent wins and small losses. Aiming for high multipliers gives rare wins and many losses. Neither approach beats the odds over time; they only change how bumpy the ride feels.

      How cashout works

      Press CASH OUT while the plane is flying. You instantly receive your stake times the current multiplier. If the round crashes before you press, the stake is gone. With two bet positions you can cash out each one independently.
